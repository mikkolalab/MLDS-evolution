# MLDS-evolution
# Overview
Childhood leukemias often begin before birth, but identifying the cell states that carry prenatal lesions to leukemia has been difficult. Myeloid leukemia of Down syndrome (ML-DS) provides a unique opportunity to track the evolution of in utero-derived malignancy due to proliferative GATA1-mutation induced perinatal pre-leukemic phase, transient abnormal myelopoiesis (TAM). Here, we integrated short- and long-read single-cell transcriptomic analysis of 50 longitudinal TAM, ML-DS and relapse samples with single-cell mutation, isoform and chromosomal copy number analyses. We defined disease stage specific signatures that distinguished malignant cells from phenotypically similar fetal progenitors, and uncovered a previously unknown hierarchy within GATA1-mutant disease. We discovered that, upstream of megakaryocyte–erythroid progenitor-like main blasts, resides a malignant progenitor state with fetal stem/progenitor cell-like transcriptional machinery and unique niche-interaction and drug-transport programs. These malignant progenitors bridged all disease stages, became prominent during low-blast phase between TAM and ML-DS and were preserved through relapse and xenotransplantation. Enhanced bridge progenitor features at ML-DS diagnosis were associated with adverse outcome in the ML-DS 2018 trial. The discovery of a stem-like, disease sustaining bridge progenitor at the apex of DS myeloid disease provides a new focal point for understanding and intervening with pediatric leukemia evolution.
# Variants calling at single cell level
Somatic mutations were tracked directly in 10x Genomics 5’ single-cell RNA-sequencing and single cell ONT long read sequencing data using a custom UMI-aware Python pipeline. Candidate variants identified by orthogonal targeted DNA sequencing were genotyped in individual cells using pileup-based interrogation of aligned BAM files using pysam. Read-level allele calls were collapsed to unique molecular identifiers (UMIs) to suppress PCR duplicates and sequencing errors, and mutation support was quantified for each cell as the fraction of ALT-supporting UMIs among all informative UMIs covering the locus. To improve recovery of large GATA1 insertions and deletions that were incompletely represented in standard Cell Ranger alignments, selected datasets were additionally processed using two-pass STAR (v2.7.10a) alignment followed by local realignment with abra2 (v2.23) prior to genotyping. Mutation calls were integrated with transcriptional profiles through shared cell barcodes and used to identify mutation-positive cells and track leukemic clones across disease stages.
